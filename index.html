Mapping EU member states

Founded in 1951, the European Coal and Steel Community was the first step in securing lasting peace in the European continent after the Second World War. In 1957, the Treaty of Rome established the European Economic Community (EEC). The Treaty was signed by six countries (Germany, France, Italy, the Netherlands, Belgium and Luxembourg) and foresaw the common management of these countries' coal and steel industries.

In 1973, Denmark, Ireland and the United Kingdom joined the EEC and were followed by Greece in 1981, and Spain and Portugal in 1986. Nine years later, and with the European Union (EU) having succeded the EEC in 1993 based on the Treaty of Maastricht, Austria, Finland and Sweden joined the EU. In 2004, ten new countries join the EU in the biggest enlargement yet: Cyprus, Czech Republic, Estonia, Hungary, Latvia, Lithuania, Malta, Poland, Slovakia, and Slovenia. Bulgaria and Romania joined the EU in 2007 and the most recent member to join was Croatia in 2013.

It has to be noted that the United Kingdom left the European Union in 2020, terminating 27 years of EU membership.
